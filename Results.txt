============================================================
INTERPRETATION AND LIMITATIONS
============================================================

- PatchTST had the strongest 7-day results in this comparison:
  lowest pooled RMSE and MAE, highest mean station NSE, and
  lower station-level RMSE at 41 of 57 stations.

- XGBoost had the lowest pooled RMSE at 30 days, narrowly
  ahead of LightGBM. Both improved pooled RMSE over persistence,
  but their mean station NSE values were lower than persistence.
  Therefore, pooled improvements do not mean every station
  benefited.

- LightGBM had the lowest pooled RMSE at 90 days, but its mean
  station NSE was strongly negative. This indicates that the
  pooled result hides poor performance at some individual wells.
  Long-horizon forecasts remain unreliable for many stations.

- Seasonal-naive baseline performance was substantially worse
  than persistence at all three horizons in this evaluation.

- The comparison uses five expanding-window rolling-origin
  folds and one seed per model/fold. Folds share much of their
  training history and should not be treated as independent
  replications.

- These are day-horizon endpoint metrics, not full-horizon
  average metrics from a single chronological split. Compare
  values only within the same horizon and sample definition.

- The dataset contains missing observations and long gaps, and
  station coverage is geographically concentrated. Results do
  not establish universal performance or deployment readiness.

- Pooled NSE is strongly influenced by between-station variation;
  interpret it alongside RMSE, mean/median station NSE, and
  station-level wins/losses.
